A connection’s port speed and its DDoS mitigation capacity answer different questions. A business may need a 1 Gbps Internet connection while facing an attack many times larger. Compare how the proposed protection keeps attack traffic away from the access circuit, what legitimate traffic can still be delivered, and what the provider commits to do during an incident.

What to ask about bundled ISP protection

Do not assume every ISP has the same protection ceiling. Some operate large scrubbing networks and automated mitigation; others offer more limited filtering. Ask for the actual protected prefixes, mitigation scope, throughput measures, blackholing policy and escalation process. A simple bandwidth multiple does not establish protection quality.

Axclusive’s Always-On option

Axclusive offers Always-On Cloudflare Magic Transit protection for DIA customers who opt in. Protected inbound traffic is routed through the mitigation service continuously, rather than waiting for an incident to trigger route activation. Axclusive-assigned protected IP addresses are Axclusive-owned. Requirements involving customer-owned prefixes or other connectivity services need a design review.

Compare the right measures

Protect services that need to stay reachable

Hosting providers may need protection for public infrastructure serving many customers. Enterprises may need it for public-facing applications, VPN gateways or business-critical Internet access. The useful outcome is a tested protection design and clear incident ownership—not a claim that every attack is eliminated.

Bring your public IP requirements, normal bandwidth, critical services and tolerance for interruption. The proposal should explain failover, monitoring, escalation and any remaining single points of failure. Retain endpoint and application security alongside network DDoS mitigation.

Questions buyers ask

Does a 1 Gbps circuit mean only 1 Gbps of attack protection? No. Attack mitigation and legitimate delivered bandwidth are separate measures. Confirm both in the service design.

Will my traffic always be faster? No. Measure important routes before and after onboarding; network paths and tunnel delivery can change latency.

Does Always-On mean no outages? No. It removes an activation step but does not eliminate every failure mode or application vulnerability. Read the agreed SLA and operating procedures.
