400G DWDM for Scalable Optical Network Infrastructure

September 16, 2026 · Axclusive · Updated 17 November 2026

400G DWDM carries a nominal 400 Gbps channel alongside other wavelengths on an optical fibre system. It can help carriers, hosting providers and enterprises increase capacity between sites without leasing a separate fibre pair for every connection. The achievable capacity and distance depend on the optics, channel spacing and complete optical path—not just the 400G label.

What is 400G DWDM?

DWDM combines optical channels on shared fibre. Coherent receivers use digital signal processing to recover the signal; forward error correction helps recover data within the specified operating limits. Neither removes the need for a suitable loss and noise budget. A 400G wavelength may carry one 400GbE client or an aggregation of lower-rate clients where the equipment supports it. Client payload capacity and gross optical line rate are not interchangeable.

Can 400G use a 50 GHz grid?

For conventional 400ZR/OpenZR+ at 400G, plan for at least 75 GHz channel spacing and a compatible optical passband. A legacy 50 GHz fixed-grid ROADM path is not a drop-in fit. Some deployments use 100 GHz spacing. Selecting every other channel on a 50 GHz system does not necessarily solve the problem: the intervening filters may still have a narrow 50 GHz passband.

This statement concerns these common 400G coherent implementations. It is not a claim that every possible coherent modulation or vendor-specific system has the same spectral requirement. Obtain qualification for the actual optics, mux/demux and ROADM chain. Wider spacing alone does not guarantee sufficient optical signal-to-noise ratio (OSNR), reach or interoperability.

For example, an assumed 4.8 THz usable spectrum window divides into 64 slots at 75 GHz or 48 at 100 GHz. If every supported channel carries nominal 400G, the idealised totals are 25.6 Tbps or 19.2 Tbps per direction. Do not calculate 96 × 400G merely because a legacy system has 96 slots at 50 GHz. Actual usable spectrum, channel widths and supported line rates must be confirmed first.

400ZR, ZR+ and optical transport equipment

400ZR targets point-to-point data-centre interconnect applications. ZR+ and other coherent transport implementations offer different reach and line-system capabilities. Longer routes can require different modulation, a reduced line rate, additional amplification or regeneration. There is no universal distance for every 400G DWDM product.

A coherent pluggable can reduce the need for a separate transponder where the router or switch supports it. Check the exact module, port power and cooling, software, FEC mode and optical design. Mux/demux equipment, amplifiers or a managed line system may still be required.

400G DWDM versus client Ethernet optics

For a short in-building connection, suitable client optics may be simpler. For capacity growth between data centres, compare DCI with a Managed Optical Fiber Network. Axclusive’s MOFN combines dark fibre, DWDM equipment and management, with protected or unprotected designs agreed for the route. For hardware-only requirements, use equipment supply.
