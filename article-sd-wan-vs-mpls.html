SD-WAN vs MPLS: Choose the Right Network for Your Sites

SD-WAN controls how traffic uses available connections; MPLS is a forwarding technology commonly used to deliver provider-managed private WAN services. They are not mutually exclusive. SD-WAN can use MPLS, Internet and mobile connections within one design.

Start with the applications your people need. A local branch accessing cloud software has different priorities from an international office exchanging data with a central application. The best choice depends on those traffic paths, service commitments and operational responsibilities.

SD-WAN vs MPLS: benefits and trade-offs

SD-WAN adds policy, visibility and path selection across available links. A managed MPLS WAN can provide private reachability and agreed traffic classes. Neither label alone tells you the end-to-end latency, availability or recovery behaviour.

DecisionManaged MPLS WANSD-WAN design
ConnectivityProvider-managed private connectivity; the core may be shared and logically separated.Uses selected Internet, private or mobile connections; MPLS can be one of them.
PerformanceAssess the offered routes, traffic classes and SLA.Assess every underlying path, steering policy and application requirement.
SecurityPrivate routing does not inherently encrypt payloads.Encryption and security features depend on the product, licensing and configuration.
CostDepends on routes, access, capacity and commitments.Include circuits, appliances, licences, security subscriptions and management.

Fortinet documents SD-WAN using multiple WAN transports, including private circuits, Internet and LTE/5G. See its SD-WAN capabilities guide.

Which approach fits your sites?

Internet-based SD-WAN for branch resilience

Consider two Internet circuits, or fixed connectivity with mobile backup, when local application access and intelligent failover are the priority. Check provider and physical route diversity: two services can share a duct, building entry or upstream dependency. Failover may interrupt sessions and depends on detection thresholds and application behaviour.

Backbone-enabled SD-WAN for international applications

Where cross-border performance matters, assess a design that carries selected traffic over a managed backbone. The benefit depends on the full route, including access at each end. Compare measured latency, loss and service commitments for your actual destinations rather than assuming every backbone path is faster.

A hybrid transition when MPLS still serves a purpose

Retain an existing MPLS service for suitable workloads while adding Internet breakout or backup paths. Pilot application policies before moving traffic or retiring contracts. You do not have to replace every site at once.

How to compare proposals

  1. List sites, application destinations and business-critical flows.
  2. Record current performance and acceptable latency, loss and interruption.
  3. Check link, CPE, power and route diversity.
  4. Agree security policies, subscriptions and management responsibilities.
  5. Test failover and recovery with representative applications.
  6. Compare total costs and contractual scope over the same period.
