What Is MPLS? Complete Guide to Multiprotocol Label Switching

MPLS—Multiprotocol Label Switching—is a forwarding technology used in provider and enterprise networks. Providers can use it to deliver private VPN services, traffic engineering and differentiated service classes. MPLS itself does not guarantee bandwidth, remove delay or encrypt your traffic; those outcomes depend on the service design and agreed commitments.

What is MPLS?

Packets are associated with forwarding classes and labels. Routers in the MPLS network use those labels to forward traffic along label-switched paths. MPLS is not a dedicated physical circuit for each customer: several customers can share the underlying infrastructure while their VPN routing and traffic remain logically separated.

How MPLS works in a network

At ingress, a router classifies traffic and applies labels. Intermediate routers swap labels; labels are removed as traffic leaves the relevant path. In a provider Layer 3 VPN, routing tables and VPN labels separate customer networks. Traffic engineering can influence paths, but paths can change during failures or network optimisation.

What MPLS can improve

A managed provider network can offer clearer operational responsibility and more controlled paths than an unmanaged combination of Internet links. QoS can give selected traffic preferential treatment during contention. It cannot create capacity or promise zero loss; policing, queue sizes and sustained overload still affect delivery. Compare the actual SLA, measurement points and exclusions.

Where MPLS services fit

Typical uses include linking offices to private applications, joining data-centre environments and providing multi-site VPN connectivity. The right choice depends on application locations, traffic patterns, access circuits and failure tolerance. Internet breakout and cloud access can be included in a design; MPLS does not inherently require all cloud traffic to return through headquarters.

Privacy, performance and operational trade-offs

A correctly operated MPLS VPN provides traffic and routing separation. It does not supply encryption, payload integrity or origin authentication by itself. Misconfiguration, compromised devices and operational access remain relevant threats. IPsec or another suitable encryption method can run over private or public transport when required.

Latency includes propagation, equipment processing and queuing. Protection switching may change the route and delay. Ordering times, price and flexibility are commercial characteristics of the chosen service, not universal properties of MPLS.

How to choose between MPLS and alternatives

SD-WAN can run over MPLS, Internet circuits or a combination. It is not necessarily a replacement for every private circuit. Bring your sites, applications and recovery requirements to Axclusive so the access and backbone options can be compared on the same basis.
