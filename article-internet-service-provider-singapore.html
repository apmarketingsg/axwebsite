An Independent Internet Service Provider in Singapore | Meet Axclusive

Choosing an internet provider in Singapore often starts with the familiar names. But for enterprises and other ISPs, the right choice also depends on how the network is actually built — where traffic travels, who operates it, and whether the service can adapt to specific requirements.

Axclusive is a Singapore internet company focused on enterprise connectivity and carrier wholesale services, including support for smaller ISPs. You may be discovering us for the first time — we're not a consumer broadband brand — but we've been in this industry for a good while, working with the network requirements behind enterprise and carrier infrastructure.

A licensed internet access provider

Axclusive Pte. Ltd. holds a Services-Based Operator (SBO) licence issued by Singapore's Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA), covering:

  • Public Internet Access Services
  • Resale of Local Leased Fixed-line Connectivity Services
  • IP Telephony Services
  • Virtual Private Network Services
  • Managed Data Network Services

For customers, this is a clear starting point for understanding who's actually delivering their connectivity: Axclusive is a licensed service provider with its own network operation and engineering responsibilities — not an unregulated middleman.

Whose network are you riding on?

It's one of the questions we hear most often from prospective customers: "Are you using Singtel, StarHub, or M1?"

The answer is no. Axclusive operates its own IP network under autonomous system number AS132337, selects its own upstream connectivity, and manages its own routing. Our service is not a rebranded retail internet package from those operators.

We connect to multiple international Tier 1 and Tier 2 providers and exchange traffic through our own peering relationships. We also use private subsea capacity between our points of presence in Singapore, Hong Kong, Marseille, and Amsterdam as part of our international backbone.

To be precise: operating independently doesn't mean owning every fibre cable or controlling the entire internet path end to end. It means we operate our own network and make our own decisions about the connectivity used to deliver our services.

The fibre underneath and the service above it

Singapore's nationwide broadband model is built around three layers, defined under IMDA's Next Gen National Broadband Network (Next Gen NBN) framework:

NetCo (e.g NetLink Trust) operates the passive fibre infrastructure underpinning Singapore's broadband network, and its wholesale model lets telecom operators use that infrastructure to deliver their own services. The RSP layer is the customer-facing role people usually associate with Singtel, StarHub, M1, ViewQwest, and MyRepublic — and Axclusive also supplies services directly to customers, with a focus on enterprises rather than residential subscribers.

Worth noting: this three-layer model was designed around residential/nationwide broadband delivery. It describes network roles, not IMDA licence categories, so it's a useful mental model rather than a literal classification of every enterprise arrangement.

Our enterprise circuits typically work a little differently from the standard broadband delivery chain: we lease the underlying fibre from the NetCo (passive infrastructure provider), then light the circuit ourselves using our own active equipment, and deliver and manage the service on top. Leasing fibre doesn't, by itself, mean reselling the fibre owner's internet service — the physical connection and the IP network carrying the traffic are different parts of the stack.

How do we compete with the big boys?

Given that Singtel, StarHub, and M1 have decades of scale and brand recognition, it's a fair question. We don't try to out-scale them — we compete differently:

  • Unique and customizable services. As an independent operator, we shape connectivity — bandwidth, routing arrangements, SLAs, service mix — around what a specific enterprise or ISP customer actually needs, rather than fitting them into a fixed retail plan.
  • Unique internet path diversity. Multiple upstream Tier 1/Tier 2 providers, our own peering, and private subsea capacity between PoPs give us real engineering choices in how traffic reaches its destination. For a business that already has connectivity from a major telco, adding Axclusive as a second, independently-operated path is genuine diversity — not just a second bill riding the same underlying network. (Performance still depends on the destination network and conditions along the path — the value is having the choices to evaluate against real requirements.)
  • Focus on enterprises and smaller ISPs, not millions of residential subscribers — meaning a team that actually knows your network.

Choosing an ISP in Singapore

When comparing internet access providers, it's worth asking: who operates the IP network, how does traffic reach the destinations that matter to your business, and what changes are possible as your requirements evolve? If you're adding a backup connection, ask specifically where the proposed service might share infrastructure with your existing one — physical routes, building entry points, and upstream networks can all affect whether two connections are truly independent.

Axclusive brings an independent network, an enterprise engineering background, and a willingness to work through those details with you.
