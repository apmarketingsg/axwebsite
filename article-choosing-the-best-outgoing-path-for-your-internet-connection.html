Choosing the Best Outgoing Path for Your Internet Connection

September 24, 2024 · Axclusive · Updated 17 November 2026

In this article
Browse all blog ↗

When you connect to the internet, your data takes a path through various networks and infrastructure to reach its destination. This path is known as the "outgoing path." Choosing an optimal outgoing path can significantly impact your internet experience regarding speed, reliability, and security. Here are some tips on selecting the best outgoing path for your needs:

Assess Your Usage and Location

Identify the destinations your business depends on: cloud regions, hosted platforms, voice services and branch offices. Record traffic volumes, peak periods and acceptable loss/latency. Most users cannot choose every Internet hop; work with your provider on available egress policies.

Evaluate Speed and Latency

Measure throughput separately from delay, loss and jitter. Use repeatable tests to the actual destination and record time, direction and load. A speed test to a nearby server does not validate overseas application performance.

Understand ISP Peering Policies

Internet service providers interconnect through peering agreements to exchange traffic. However, not all providers peer directly. Your data may take an indirect route leading to congestion and delays. Research your ISP's peering policies and confirm they peer directly with key networks like top content delivery systems. If your ISP has poor peering arrangements, compare tested provider paths; a VPN may change the route but can also add overhead and latency.

Configure Your Outbound Route

Once you decide on an ISP or third-party routing service, optimize your outbound traffic flow through their configuration options. Most home routers have basic WAN settings, but business routers or load balancers allow binding traffic to specific outbound IPs or network interfaces. BFD detects forwarding failures; it does not choose the fastest egress route. Routing or SD-WAN policy makes that choice. Work with your ISP or network administrator to assign routing rules and policies tailored to your usage and destination networks.

Consider Caching and CDNs

CDNs can bring an organisation’s cacheable content closer to its users. As a visitor, changing DNS does not let you select or configure another company’s CDN. Site owners can configure an appropriate CDN or multi-CDN policy and measure real-user outcomes.

Secure Your Connection

While optimizing performance, don't neglect security when picking an outbound path. Use appropriately configured encrypted VPNs where required; an ordinary proxy does not necessarily encrypt traffic, so your traffic is not exposed on shared networks. Check if your ISP blocks insecure ports or protocols. For site owners, enable HTTPS encryption and use reputable DDoS protection services to safeguard infrastructure against attacks. Monitoring tools can also analyze outbound traffic for anomalies indicating breaches.

Test and Tweak Over Time

Keep testing connection metrics like bitrates and latencies while using internet-heavy applications. Note any dips during peak periods as congestion can clog up previously fast routes. Consistently monitor traffic via router statistics to catch poorly performing outbound links. As usage changes or new infrastructure emerges, reassess paths to maintain an optimal user experience. Consider scheduling tests during daytime, nighttime and weekends.

Choosing your internet connection's best outgoing path involves considering multiple factors. Mix and match different providers, protocols, and tools as required. It can help you build an outgoing route for optimal speed, reliability, and security based on your usage patterns, applications, and location. Pay attention as performance degrades and be ready to reconfigure as necessary. With a thoughtful approach, you can achieve a smooth user experience regardless of your inbound bandwidth.
