Choose an office phone system around how your business handles calls, then select the platform and voice connection. An on-site PBX, Axclusive Cloud PBX, Microsoft Teams Phone and Webex Calling can support different working environments; the useful comparison includes call flows, licences, telephone numbers and who supports the complete service.

At the start of a working day, a caller may need sales, an existing customer may need support and a colleague may be away from their desk. Before comparing licences, decide where each call should go and what happens when nobody answers.

Separate the phone system from the connection

The phone system manages the calling experience. The voice connection links the agreed system to external telephone calling. Buying a software subscription does not automatically settle the connection, number migration, devices or support arrangements.

Axclusive is an IMDA-licensed IP telephony provider. We can supply the SIP trunk and manage an agreed cloud-based PBX solution, bringing the voice connection and phone system into one service discussion.

On-site does not always mean a separate gateway. A compatible IP PBX can connect to a SIP trunk directly; a gateway is needed when the chosen design or legacy analogue/ISDN equipment requires it. The on-site server or VM and its upkeep still need an owner.

Included does not mean unlimited. For Axclusive Cloud PBX, the proposal sets the included capacity and usage charges. Number porting is charged separately. For all four options, compare the same users, concurrent calls, destinations, devices, support hours and contract period.

If you want one provider for the phone system and voice connection, start with Axclusive Cloud PBX. If users already work in Teams or Webex, ask us to compare the incremental licensing and voice costs against the cloud PBX option. Write down the caller’s journey

Start with a simple worksheet: the number dialled, who should answer, the working hours and the fallback destination. Include holidays and situations where the intended person is unavailable.

A hosted PBX can be configured around call-routing requirements. Yeastar’s documented inbound routing supports destinations based on criteria such as numbers and time conditions. Confirm the required features for the selected edition and service rather than assuming every feature is included. Yeastar inbound-route documentation

An illustrative small-office design might send the main number to reception during business hours and to an agreed alternative outside those hours. That is a planning example, not a promise that every configuration is included in a standard package.

Count simultaneous calls as well as people

Twenty staff do not necessarily require twenty concurrent external calls. Equally, a small team handling busy inbound lines can need more capacity than its headcount suggests. Discuss expected simultaneous calls, destinations and busy periods alongside user licences.

Use the existing SIP spending calculator to explore the published SIP pricing. The complete voice proposal may also include platform licences, devices, migration and other agreed components. Number porting is charged separately.

Plan the changeover before changing the numbers

Record the numbers to retain, existing service details, device requirements and target date. Agree who coordinates porting, configures the system and supports users at cutover. Test inbound and outbound calls, transfers, caller identity and the agreed call flows before signing off the migration.

Connectivity is part of the design. Review the office network and Internet access, including what users should do if the normal connection fails. A cloud platform does not remove every dependency at the customer site.

Know what support means

For Axclusive’s managed Yeastar PBX, standard support is 8×5 with a four-hour response target. Optional 24×7 coverage with a four-hour response target is available. A response target is not a guaranteed resolution time. Confirm the service hours and the responsibilities included in the proposal.
