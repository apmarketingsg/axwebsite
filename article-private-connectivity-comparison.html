Private connectivity: leased line, DCI, IPLC/IEPL or MOFN?

The right private connection depends on what you need to connect, where the endpoints are and who will operate the network. Start with those requirements, then choose between a leased line, data centre interconnect, an international private line or a managed optical network.

Consider a business with an office in Singapore, servers in a local data centre and an application environment overseas. This is an illustrative example, not a customer case. The office-to-data-centre path and the international path solve different problems—even if both are described as private connectivity.

Choose the service around your endpoints

Your requirementStart withWhat to confirm
Connect offices, buildings or an office and a data centre within SingaporeLeased lineExact addresses, bandwidth, handoff and availability
Connect two data centre environmentsData Centre Interconnect (DCI)Facilities, rack handoffs, cross-connects and workload requirements
Connect locations in different countriesIPLC/IEPLBoth endpoints, international route, capacity, handoff and protection
Obtain dark fibre, DWDM equipment and ongoing management togetherManaged Optical Fiber Network (MOFN)Fibre design, equipment, growth and operating responsibilities

These labels describe different aspects of the requirement. DCI identifies the data-centre-to-data-centre use case; it does not, by itself, specify the underlying transport technology. An international connection can also join two data centres.

When a Singapore leased line fits

A leased line provides an agreed private connection between locations. It can link an office to another building or a business site to a data centre. It is a different requirement from providing users with public Internet access.

Bring both addresses and the applications that depend on the connection. A file-transfer workflow, a branch application and a replication process may need different capacity or continuity arrangements. Axclusive offers Singapore-wide connectivity, with availability and delivery confirmed for each address.

See leased lines and private connectivity.

When to ask for DCI

Ask for DCI when you need to join data centre environments. Hosting providers may connect parts of their platform; enterprises may connect primary and recovery systems; ISPs may connect network infrastructure.

The circuit is only part of the delivery. Identify where each end must terminate, who orders the facility cross-connect and how the service will be tested. Do not leave the rack-to-provider handoff until the intended activation date.

IPLC and IEPL: ask about the actual delivery

For international requirements, the service name alone is not enough. Ask for the handoff, reserved capacity, route, protection design and service commitments in writing.

Axclusive offers 10G and 100G international private connectivity, with Ethernet or optical transport handoffs agreed for the requirement. Our offered 10G IPLC design carries reserved capacity at the optical layer. That statement describes our service design; it should not be assumed to describe every provider’s 10G product.

The reserved Layer 1 allocation is dedicated transport capacity, rather than a shared packet service whose bandwidth is divided between customers by QoS. Your own routers and applications can still need traffic management if their demand exceeds the purchased capacity. A predictable transport path also does not mean zero latency or an identical delay under every fault condition: a protection switch can change the path.

Ethernet handoff and optical transport describe different parts of the design. The customer-facing interface does not, on its own, prove what transport is used underneath. IEPL describes an international Ethernet private-line service; IPLC is a broader international private-circuit term. Ask for the actual service attributes rather than assuming these names imply identical designs. MEF also defines distinct Ethernet service types, reinforcing why the agreed attributes matter more than a loose label. MEF Ethernet service definitions

When MOFN is the better discussion

MOFN brings the dark fibre connection, DWDM optical equipment and managed service together. It suits a conversation about optical capacity and ongoing responsibility, rather than buying a circuit and separately coordinating its equipment and operation.

Axclusive offers protected or unprotected options. Discuss the route and equipment design, expansion needs and what happens during a fault. Read about Managed Optical Fiber Network.

Decide how much interruption the workload can tolerate

“Protected” and “two circuits” need more detail. Ask which elements are independent, whether routes share physical infrastructure, how a failure is detected and what service commitments apply. Agree the design against the application’s tolerance for interruption.

Send a requirement that can be quoted

Provide both endpoints, desired capacity, interface requirements, current services, target date and protected or unprotected preference. Include the workload and any latency or continuity requirement. That gives the engineering team a useful starting point.
