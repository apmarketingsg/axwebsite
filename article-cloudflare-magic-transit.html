What Is Cloudflare Magic Transit?

March 19, 2026 · Axclusive · Updated 17 November 2026

Cloudflare Magic Transit protects IP networks against network-layer DDoS attacks by receiving traffic through Cloudflare’s network before delivering permitted traffic to the customer. This guide explains the routing and delivery choices; Axclusive’s DDoS protection page describes the service available with our connectivity.

What does Magic Transit protect?

Magic Transit provides network-layer protection, including L3/L4 DDoS mitigation. It is not automatically a website reverse proxy, CDN, WAF or bot-management subscription. HTTP application protection may need separately scoped Cloudflare application services. A firewall at the customer site still has a role.

How the traffic path works

Protected prefixes are announced through Cloudflare. Internet routing selects a reachable anycast entry point according to BGP policy and network topology—not necessarily the physically nearest city or the lowest-latency path. Permitted traffic is then delivered through the configured tunnels or interconnects.

GRE encapsulates traffic but does not encrypt it by itself. IPsec can provide encryption when configured. Cloudflare Network Interconnect is a separate connectivity option; available delivery methods must be confirmed for the proposed service. Anycast endpoints simplify Cloudflare-side connectivity, but do not remove the need for customer-side redundancy.

Protection scope and addressing

Axclusive offers Always-On Magic Transit protection to DIA customers who opt in. For Axclusive-assigned protected addresses, the address space belongs to Axclusive—not Cloudflare. Customer-owned prefixes require an eligibility and routing review. Cloudflare tunnel endpoint addressing is a separate technical matter and does not change ownership of the customer’s service addresses.

Compare commitments, not labels

Providers differ in mitigation capacity, automation and escalation policy. There is no universal rule that ISP protection is limited to ten times a circuit’s bandwidth. Ask what happens when attacks exceed the contracted design, when blackholing may occur, and how restoration is handled. Global aggregate capacity is not bandwidth dedicated to one customer.

Ingress-only and egress designs

With ingress-only protection and Direct Server Return, return traffic uses the customer’s normal upstream path. An egress-enabled design can also route outbound traffic through Cloudflare where supported and agreed. This is a routing choice, not an HTTP “full proxy” mode. Always-On versus On-Demand describes when protection routing is active; it is a different choice from ingress versus egress.

Validate performance before activation

Test important source markets, application flows, tunnel MTU, path changes and failover. Inspection, encapsulation and routing can change delay; faster delivery or zero interruption cannot be guaranteed for every path. Agree the clean-traffic capacity, SLA, monitoring and incident responsibilities in the proposal.
