From a single provider to a coordinated service

Your customers experience the journey,
not the CDN contract.

Managed multi-CDN brings two or more content delivery networks into one operating plan. Smart DNS directs users towards eligible providers, while real-user monitoring (RUM) helps identify where delivery needs to improve.

A hosting customer in Malaysia and an enterprise buyer in Thailand may experience the same website differently. Instead of choosing one provider for every market, Axclusive helps you adapt delivery to measured conditions, your contracted services and your business priorities.

DNS steering sets targets for DNS answers, not an exact percentage of every request or byte. Resolver caching, TTLs and existing connections affect the observed split and how quickly changes take effect.

Resilience needs a complete design

A 50/50 split keeps both paths in use.

Under healthy conditions, balanced delivery supports an active-active approach: both contracted CDNs carry traffic rather than leaving one path unused.

High availability also depends on working DNS, ready CDN capacity, consistent certificates and configuration, tested health policies and a resilient origin. A traffic split alone cannot guarantee application availability.

Make switching safe for the application

Align domain onboarding, TLS certificates, cache rules, content invalidation, origin access and any contracted security policies across providers. Test cookies, sessions and signed content where relevant.

CDN delivery does not replace network-layer DDoS protection. Scope WAF and application security separately from the delivery policy.

Managed CDN FAQ

Clear answers about multi-CDN, RUM and Smart DNS.

What is managed multi-CDN?+
Managed multi-CDN combines two or more contracted content delivery networks under a coordinated delivery policy. Axclusive manages Smart DNS traffic steering using real-user measurements, geography and agreed performance and availability criteria.
How does RUM influence CDN selection?+
Real-user monitoring (RUM) measures the delivery experience of actual users. Axclusive uses the agreed measurements and evaluation window to inform which eligible CDN should receive more traffic in each market. Adequate sample quality and a defined fallback policy matter when data is limited.
Are 80/20 and 50/50 fixed allocations?+
No. These are example target weights agreed for your service. A healthy baseline can use 50/50, while a peak-hour policy can favour the better-performing provider with 80/20. DNS caching and shared resolvers mean observed traffic volumes will not exactly match these weights.
Does Smart DNS move every active session immediately?+
No. Smart DNS changes the CDN returned by eligible DNS lookups. Cached DNS answers, TTLs and existing connections affect when users move. Failover timing and application behaviour must be tested for the proposed configuration.
Can enterprises use the service as well as hosting providers?+
Yes. Hosting providers can use managed multi-CDN for customer websites, assets and downloads. Enterprises can use it for e-commerce, campaign sites and customer-facing applications. Cache rules and application compatibility are assessed for each workload.
Does multi-CDN replace DDoS protection?+
No. CDN delivery and failover do not replace a complete security design. WAF, application DDoS controls and network-layer protection have distinct scopes. Axclusive offers optional Always-On Cloudflare Magic Transit protection with its DIA service for agreed IP addresses.
Which CDN providers and support commitments are included?+
The available provider mix, contracts, domains, regions, traffic allowances, reporting and support commitments are confirmed in your proposal. Cloudflare and Akamai are illustrative examples here, not a promise that every plan includes both.
