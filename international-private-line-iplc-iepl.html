IPLC vs IEPL: choosing the handoff

Ethernet or OTU handoff. Your choice, confirmed in the design.

IPLC means International Private Leased Circuit. IEPL means International Ethernet Private Line and describes an Ethernet private-line service. Provider terminology varies, so the proposal should specify the interface, transport and service behaviour.

01

Ethernet handoff

Connect suitable Ethernet equipment using the agreed service presentation. Confirm port speed, optics, frame size and any VLAN or transparency requirements for your IEPL design.

02

OTU handoff

Connect suitable Optical Transport Network equipment through an agreed Optical Transport Unit interface. Share the required OTU type, optics and equipment details for compatibility checks.

03

Confirm both endpoints

Tell us the interface required at each end. Handoff, mapping, demarcation and testing are confirmed together; do not assume an arbitrary mix of interfaces is supported.

A closer look at 10G delivery

Dedicated 10G capacity at the optical layer.

Axclusive’s 10G IPLC provides dedicated capacity over Layer 1 optical transport. Your circuit has its own reserved bandwidth, which other customers cannot consume.

Several services can use the same optical transport system while retaining separate capacity allocations. Your reserved 10G bandwidth is not a contended pool that other customers consume, and you do not need to buy the whole higher-capacity circuit.

Dedicated capacity

Your allocated 10G transport capacity remains available to your circuit. Carrying multiple circuits over the same optical infrastructure does not mean sharing your allocated bandwidth.

No packet QoS needed on the Layer 1 path

The optical transport does not use packet-priority queues to allocate your reserved capacity. QoS may still be useful on your routers or LAN if your own traffic exceeds a link’s capacity or needs application prioritisation.

Stable latency on a fixed path

The Layer 1 transport avoids congestion-driven packet queuing. Delay is predictable for the selected route and equipment; a protection switch or restoration onto a different path can change it. End-to-end application latency also includes your equipment.

Before you choose a circuit

International Private Line FAQ

Start with the endpoints, then confirm the service design.

When should I choose an IPLC or IEPL instead of Internet connectivity?+
Choose a private international line when you need defined transport capacity between agreed overseas endpoints, such as data centres, hosting platforms or network POPs. IEPL suits an agreed Ethernet handoff. For general Internet access or many changing destinations, consider DIA or IP transit; Managed SD-WAN can coordinate application paths across suitable connections. Share your endpoints and traffic requirements so Axclusive can assess the fit.
Is the 10G IPLC bandwidth shared with other customers?+
No. For Axclusive’s Layer 1 optical 10G IPLC, your circuit has reserved Layer 1 capacity. Multiple circuits may share the transport infrastructure, but other customers do not consume your allocated 10G bandwidth.
Does the optical IPLC require QoS, and is latency stable?+
Packet QoS is not required to secure reserved capacity on the Layer 1 optical path. A fixed transport path has predictable delay without congestion-driven packet queuing. Customer equipment, traffic exceeding an endpoint link’s capacity, or protection switching to another route can still affect end-to-end performance.
What is the difference between IPLC and IEPL?+
IPLC describes an international private leased circuit. IEPL highlights an Ethernet private-line service. Axclusive offers Ethernet or OTU handoff; the proposal confirms the transport design and interface requirements.
Do you provide 10G and 100G services?+
Yes. Axclusive provides 10G and 100G international private-line services over various submarine cable systems. Availability and delivery details are confirmed for your endpoints.
Can I choose protected or unprotected connectivity?+
Yes. Tell us which you require. We assess the available design and agree protection arrangements, dependencies and service commitments for the proposed circuit.
Is an international private line the same as IP transit?+
No. A private line provides transport between agreed endpoints. IP transit provides Internet reach through routing. They can be combined where the network design requires both.
Is private connectivity automatically encrypted?+
No. Private transport does not itself establish end-to-end encryption. Share your encryption requirements so they can be considered in the design.
Which cable systems and routes are available?+
Send the A-end and B-end locations, capacity, handoff and protection requirements. Axclusive will confirm available cable and route options for the proposed service.
How is the price determined?+
A quotation depends on endpoints, route availability, capacity, handoffs, protection, access links, cross-connects and contract term. The proposal states what is included and any additional delivery requirements.
