AI Training vs. AI Inference: Compute, Storage and Network Requirements

Training adjusts a model’s learned parameters; inference uses a trained model to produce outputs. Both can depend on compute, memory, storage and network performance. Their requirements vary with model size, parallelism, batching and user demand.

Start with the workload

A small model may run on one CPU or GPU, while a large model may use many accelerators. Training is often batch-oriented; inference can be interactive or batch-oriented. Avoid assuming every training job is distributed or every inference request is small and latency-sensitive.

What is AI training?

Neural-network training commonly involves forward and backward computation and parameter updates across batches. An epoch is a pass over the training dataset, not one forward/backward step. Distributed training may exchange gradients, activations or parameters depending on its design. Communication delays can reduce throughput; a network interruption does not automatically corrupt the model. Checkpointing and recovery limit lost work.

What is AI inference?

Inference generates outputs from a trained model. Interactive applications need acceptable response time, while batch jobs may prioritise throughput and cost. Large models can require substantial communication between GPUs or servers during inference; it is not solely traffic between a server and the end user.

Compare both internal and external paths

  • Training: measure data ingestion and accelerator synchronisation alongside compute utilisation.
  • Inference: measure queueing, computation, memory access, internal communication and network delay to users.
  • Placement: compare regional latency, capacity, cost and data-location requirements. Distributing a workload is not always the most efficient choice.

Identify the bottleneck rather than assuming it

A faster WAN will not repair slow storage or an overloaded inference engine. Equally, spare GPU capacity cannot overcome a constrained data pipeline. Measure the individual stages and test the actual model before selecting bandwidth.

Frequently asked questions

Does inference need less networking? Not always. Multi-GPU or disaggregated inference can be communication-intensive.

Does training tolerate latency? Some bulk transfers do; frequent synchronisation in tightly coupled training can be very latency-sensitive.

Which costs more? Compare the training schedule with expected inference volume, model, hardware and utilisation. There is no universal lifetime-cost winner.
