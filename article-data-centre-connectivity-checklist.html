Singapore data centre connectivity: what to check before ordering

Planning data centre connectivity means specifying the full path from your equipment to the systems and users it needs to reach. Confirm the facility handoff, cross-connect responsibilities, bandwidth and resilience before ordering the external circuit.

A server can be ready while its connection is not. A team may have selected a rack and arranged equipment delivery, only to discover that the provider handoff or facility cross-connect still needs coordination. Treat those dependencies as part of the project from the beginning.

Start with the workload and destination

Ask where the traffic goes and what happens if the path is unavailable. That gives a better starting point than choosing a port speed alone.

Destination or taskConnection to assess
Public Internet users and external networksIP transit for suitable network requirements, or dedicated Internet access
Another data centre environmentData Centre Interconnect
An office or building in SingaporePrivate leased line
An overseas site or data centreInternational private line, with the endpoints and route agreed
A supported cloud environmentPrivate cloud connectivity, subject to destination availability
Multiple service destinations at one siteMSSC, with a suitable handoff and resilience design

A hosting platform might need both public Internet reach and a private connection to another facility. An enterprise might need office access, cloud connectivity and a separate recovery path. These are different traffic requirements and should be specified separately.

Separate the cross-connect from the external service

A cross-connect is a physical connection between agreed endpoints, typically within a data centre. Connections between buildings or campuses can require a different product. It is not automatically the same purchase as an external circuit. For example, Equinix describes its Cross Connects as dedicated physical cabling between parties within an Equinix IBX data centre. The order and handoff requirements depend on the facility. Equinix cross-connect documentation

For each end, identify the facility, equipment location, interface and provider handoff. Agree which party orders and pays for the cross-connect and which authorisations are needed. Confirm when the physical connection will be ready relative to the external service.

Do not assume one facility’s ordering rules apply everywhere.

Specify bandwidth in the right place

Record the capacity of the access circuit, each service carried over it and the interfaces used by your equipment. Those figures can differ.

For Axclusive’s Multiple Services Single Circuit offering, the access circuit can be 1G to 100G, while private cloud connection bandwidth is selected separately—typically 1 Mbps to 10 Gbps, subject to the destination and service. A 100G access circuit does not mean every connected cloud service has a 100G allocation.

Review MSSC and private cloud connectivity when combining destinations. Confirm the cloud region and exact service rather than relying only on a provider’s brand name.

Ask what the backup path actually avoids

Two connections are useful only when their design addresses the failures you are concerned about. Ask whether the intended paths share a building entry, physical route, equipment or another dependency. Then decide how traffic should move when one fails.

For replication or recovery workloads, discuss expected traffic and recovery objectives with the application owner. A backup path is not a substitute for testing that the application can operate over it.

Keep space, power and connectivity availability separate

A connectivity directory is a starting point for an address enquiry. It is not a live reservation of rack space, power or capacity, and does not prove a reseller relationship with every facility listed.

Axclusive offers Singapore-wide connectivity, subject to address confirmation. Use the Singapore network coverage and data centre directory to begin the location discussion. If you also require a place for the equipment, assess server and network colocation separately with the space, power and operating requirements.

Agree the acceptance checks

Before activation, agree what will demonstrate that the service is ready. The checklist should cover the handoff, addressing or routing requirements, purchased capacity, reachability and any agreed protection test. Confirm who attends the test and who handles a fault across the facility and network boundaries.

An illustrative enterprise project could start by connecting a primary and recovery environment, then test the intended replication flow before moving production traffic. This is a planning example, not a reported customer deployment or a guaranteed recovery result.

What to include in your enquiry

Send both endpoint addresses, facilities and equipment locations where known; the required service and bandwidth; interface details; the applications or networks being connected; and the target date. Include cross-connect status and continuity requirements. If a detail is unknown, mark it for confirmation rather than guessing.
