What is multi-service connectivity?

One access circuit.
Your choice of service ports.

Multiple Services Single Circuit (MSSC) is Axclusive’s multi-service connectivity offering: Internet access, private cloud connectivity and private Ethernet share one access circuit while remaining logically separated. At your premises, choose a single VLAN-based handoff or customer premises equipment (CPE) that presents each agreed service on a separate physical port.

MSSC describes combining services on a shared access circuit; it does not limit your solution to one circuit. Where resilience is required, Axclusive can deliver dual circuits on request. We agree capacity, routing, service separation and the failover design around your requirements.

Private cloud access within your multi-service design

Bring your cloud connection together with the rest of your network.

An office can need Internet access, private cloud applications and a connection to its data centre at the same time. MSSC brings those requirements into one access design.

Choose your cloud destination

Discuss AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Alibaba Cloud and Tencent Cloud connections. Oracle Cloud, IBM Cloud / SoftLayer, Huawei Cloud, Kingsoft Cloud and UCloud can also be assessed on enquiry. All destinations are subject to availability, region and the agreed delivery arrangement.

Allocate capacity to each service

Cloud connection capacity is agreed separately from site access. Where MSSC is used, its 1G–100G access circuit carries the combined services; individual cloud connection options can range from 1 Mbps to 10 Gbps, subject to provider, region and delivery design. A 100G access circuit does not provide a 100G connection to each cloud.

Fit the connection to your equipment

Use one port with separated services, or CPE breakout for separate cloud, Internet and private Ethernet ports. If continuity matters, request dual circuits and review the complete backup design.

Cloud-account configuration, routing responsibilities, encryption and cloud-provider charges are confirmed in the proposal. Multiple ports alone do not create independent backup paths.

During a data centre or cloud migration, your current systems and new environment may need to operate together. Plan the transfer path alongside normal Internet traffic.

Plan around: Agree peak transfer requirements, change windows and the steps to remove temporary connections.

Scenario 04

Backups that must finish before morning

Production traffic continues while backups or replication move to a second site or cloud destination. Size and separate the services around both workloads.

Plan around: Define the backup window, simultaneous traffic, recovery needs and any configured service policies.

Plan around: Confirm port types, VLAN mapping, equipment ownership and the support boundary.

A platform adding another destination

An expanding application may need another cloud or private endpoint. Review whether the existing access capacity and handoff can support the addition.

Plan around: Check feasibility, available capacity and provisioning requirements before ordering a new circuit.

Fit the handoff to your equipment

Single circuit does not mean single port.

The circuit brings the services to your site. Customer premises equipment (CPE) can then break them out onto separate physical ports, with each port assigned to a different service or purpose. Choose the handoff that fits your firewall, routers and switches.

Single-port handoff

Use a VLAN-aware device to receive the agreed services on one physical port. This can suit teams that manage their own service separation and routing.

Discuss: VLAN tags, interface type, service bandwidths, routing responsibilities and the aggregate port limit.

Separate service ports at your site

Use CPE to break out one incoming circuit into separate service ports. For example, connect an Internet port to your firewall, a private cloud port to your cloud-facing router, and a private Ethernet port to equipment serving another office or data centre. The port assignments are agreed for your design.

Discuss: Number and type of ports, VLAN mapping, CPE capacity, management and equipment support.

Multiple CPE ports still share the upstream access circuit. They are not independent physical paths.

Simplify delivery. Design resilience deliberately.

Need resilience? Request dual circuits.

Axclusive can deliver dual circuits on request for link resilience and high availability (HA). Keep the benefits of multi-service connectivity while planning a second circuit for continuity if a link fails.

For critical workloads, agree route and building-entry diversity where available, equipment, power, cloud connections and failover behaviour. Two circuits can still share infrastructure, so the proposal must identify shared risks and the resilience the design provides.

A resilient cloud interconnect does not, by itself, make the customer’s last-mile connection resilient. Test the complete path against the agreed requirements.

Multiple Services Single Circuit (MSSC) FAQ

Is MSSC the same as private cloud connectivity?+
MSSC is the access design that combines services on a circuit; private cloud connectivity is one service it can carry. MSSC access is available from 1G to 100G. Cloud connection bandwidth is selected separately, typically from 1 Mbps to 10 Gbps subject to the destination and service. A 100G access circuit does not imply a 100G connection to every cloud.
Is Cloud Connect the same as multi-service connectivity?+
No. Cloud Connect describes connectivity to a cloud environment. Multi-service connectivity describes delivering several services through common physical access. A private cloud connection can be one of those services alongside Internet and private Ethernet. Explore our Cloud Connectivity service for cloud-specific requirements.
Which clouds can I connect to?+
Discuss AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Alibaba Cloud and Tencent Cloud connections. Oracle Cloud, IBM Cloud / SoftLayer, Huawei Cloud, Kingsoft Cloud and UCloud can also be assessed on enquiry. All destinations are subject to availability, region and the agreed delivery arrangement. Specify cloud regions, accounts, workloads and the intended connection type so configuration can be confirmed.
Does a single circuit mean I only get one port?+
No. The circuit is the access connection to your premises; the ports are the physical interfaces your equipment connects to. Axclusive can use CPE to break out Internet, private cloud and private Ethernet services onto separate ports, each with an agreed purpose. A single-port VLAN handoff is also available. Port numbers, types, bandwidth and service assignments are confirmed in the proposal.
What speeds are available?+
Cloud connection capacity is agreed separately from site access. Where MSSC is used, its 1G–100G access circuit carries the combined services; individual cloud connection options can range from 1 Mbps to 10 Gbps, subject to provider, region and delivery design. A 100G access circuit does not provide a 100G connection to each cloud. Aggregate service demand must fit the agreed physical capacity and service policies.
Does a private cloud connection include encryption?+
Not automatically. Private routing and encryption are different controls. Confirm encryption requirements, supported methods and responsibility for configuration as part of the design.
Can I add a service to an existing circuit?+
Ask the team to check spare capacity, interface capability, service availability and configuration. An additional service may be possible without new physical access, but this must be confirmed for the existing circuit.
Do I keep control of my routing?+
Customer-controlled routing is supported by the existing service model. Agree the handoff, VLANs, routing boundaries and security responsibilities. Additional management can be discussed as a separate scope.
Can I request dual circuits for resilience or HA?+
Yes. Axclusive can deliver dual circuits on request for link resilience and high availability (HA). We confirm access-path diversity, shared infrastructure, equipment, capacity and failover behaviour in the proposed design. Separate service ports on one CPE are not a substitute for circuit redundancy.
