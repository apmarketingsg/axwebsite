What global coverage means

More than four points on a map.

Axclusive’s backbone-enabled SD-WAN is not limited to our four core POPs. Where suitable virtual-machine infrastructure is available within the extended footprint, we can assess a virtual SD-WAN gateway to bring additional locations into the proposed network.

A city listing is a starting point for service planning, not a promise of an installed Axclusive POP or guaranteed access at every building. We confirm the gateway, local connectivity, routing, licensing and delivery scope against your requirements.

Choose the right connection behind each site

Internet-based SD-WAN

Combine suitable internet links with application-aware steering and failover. A practical option when local internet access meets the workload’s needs. Hybrid designs can also incorporate existing MPLS links.

Backbone-enabled SD-WAN

Use selected provider backbone routes for inter-site traffic where the design benefits from greater routing control. Review latency, packet loss and resilience for the agreed path; a backbone does not automatically guarantee better performance for every destination.

Global SD-WAN coverage FAQ

Where can Axclusive deliver global SD-WAN?+
Axclusive operates four core POPs in Singapore, Hong Kong, Marseille and Amsterdam, with an extended compute footprint covering 58 unique cities. We assess virtual gateway availability, connectivity and local access for each proposed site before confirming delivery.
Are the 58 cities additional to the four core POPs?+
No. Singapore, Hong Kong, Marseille and Amsterdam are included in the 58-city footprint. The total is 58 unique cities, not 62.
Can you connect a branch outside a listed city?+
Ask us to assess the exact address. A branch may connect over suitable local access to an available gateway or core POP. A listed city does not guarantee service at every address, and an unlisted address is not automatically excluded.
Does every branch need a private leased line?+
No. Internet-based SD-WAN can use suitable internet connections. Backbone-enabled designs combine branch access with selected provider backbone routes. We assess the access and transport required for each application.
Can this replace an international MPLS network?+
It can be considered as an MPLS alternative or part of a phased migration. We compare application performance, routing, resilience, security and contractual requirements before recommending a design.
Is latency or availability guaranteed across every location?+
No universal latency or uptime commitment applies to the entire map. Performance targets, redundancy, service boundaries and any SLA are defined for the quoted design and agreed routes.
